The Earthworm

The Earthworm on the ground, let him be, Playing jolly and wandering free. He likes it when it rains, you see. He does not have as many years as you and I, To see the rains when the monsoon comes by. He is innocent and humble; he is not vain. He just likes to play in the rain. He wants to cause no one, no harm nor pain. He does not wish to conquer nor dominate you, Then why are you, threatened by him too? The Earthworm, he does not mind If he’s not the brownest of his kind, Or the longest of his kin. So why do you care then To be the strongest in men? He is thriving and so are you. His joy does not belittle your joy too. He likes to play in the rains, you see, Playing jolly and wandering free. The Earthworm on the ground, let him be.

Rains

The mighty sun when fails to rule the skies, The blazing, scorching, troubling heat then dies. As clouds engulf the sun and take the rule, And winds begin to howl, so pleasing cool. From heavens now emerge the droplets first, Watering the plants, quenching the birds’ thirst. The tiny droplets on my head then fall, Overwhelming me, the droplets so small. The trees then dance and sway, the winds when blow. The drizzle starts so pleasing, soothing slow. The clouds upon the air then roar and grumble. The drizzle starts so light and soft and humble. Now do arise and waft the sweetest smells Of soil and earth, as joy in heart mine wells. Now does arise the fragrance of the flowers, Enchanting and entrancing in these showers. The rule of darker, mightier clouds then reigns. Then the earth is blessed by heavier rains. The musical sound of drops hitting the ground Leaves me enchanted, utterly spellbound. The drops of water falling with a splash, The distant boom of thunder, lightning flash, The gusts of winds so moist and mighty strong Appeal and lull me, as I sit idle for long.

The Sound of a Waterfall

I was happy, comfortable, and at rest, When suddenly I found myself in a forest. Distressed, frantically I ran about The forest, but there was no way out. In dense forests I had to walk All alone with no one to talk. Darkness, desperation, dread, despair Haunted me for long, and I wasn’t prepared. It was dark, not a ray of sunlight. I was thirsty and there wasn’t water in sight. In oceans of despair I had drowned, When out of nowhere a place I found. Then the sweetest musical sound I heard, A rustle of leaves, the chirp of a bird, The thunder of water falling from a height, Crashing over the rocks, forming foam snow-white. The waterfall comforted me, soothed my soul. It refreshed and rejuvenated me as a whole. It brought me relief, it brought me hope. Gradually, with the forest, I learnt to cope. They always come, the darker days. But them, you will have to learn to face. Now fight that fear, wipe that tear. Now smile, face that danger with a cheer.

Enlightened

I walked alone along the dark And twisty road for long; Until there was no soul in me, No heart to move along. I crept in this eternity Away from human throng. I trudged this dark and dreary world, To which I then belonged. The cold now crept along my back, The cold now struck my heart. In journey mine as I shivered, Away from world, depart. The sound of steps of mine heavy, The thumping of my heart, Was all I heard when I had walked Then from the world apart. Dark thoughts then gathered in my head, That clashed and caused a mess. My thoughts so deep and scary dark My thoughts of fear and stress. The gloomy thoughts then in my head In silence so intense Got amplified a hundredfold Upon the air so tense. But oh! How long could I have walked Like this, afraid and sad. I looked around and saw myself That it was not so bad. My head now felt so fresh and clean; My head now felt so light. As I stood up, rejuvenated Into the world so bright. As joy, relief, delight and peace, Then filled my thoughts, content I saw the world around me change The world now so pleasant. To not, then, see the brighter side And light; had I been blind? Had all the darkness just been part Of fantasy of mind?

Being

Come with me, Child, and I’ll show you the way. Just take my hand, and I’ll take you away. No, Father! What if I want to stay? I don’t want to go, not me today. Oh, my Child! You have to give in. To not want to come, is itself a sin. Don’t take me there, don’t start me again. I don’t want to go, or I’ll go insane. You have to, Child! You must stay strong. To not want to live, is itself very wrong. But there are men down there, to corrupt my soul, To spoil and taint my heart as a whole. There is hunger and wealth, poverty and fame, Luxury and destitution, honour and shame. There is hatred and strife, war and aggression, Cold hostility and fiery passion. There is fear and anger, envy and greed. Don’t take me there again, I plead. Why, Father, do you want me to be? Why, Father, must I live, and not be free? I understand, my Child, Life brings grief. But there are ways to find relief. Down there, what lies is not entirely Hell. There are places where some Hope does dwell. You go there, not to take what’s there. You go there to give them their share. You go there, not to assume their ways. You go there to purify and cleanse that place. You go there, not to become a part of them. You go there, to demonstrate and condemn. You go, because you are granted a chance To rectify all wrongs and sins there, perchance. It is not a punishment, Child, it is a gift. There’s a motive in being, lest you want to stay adrift. Now go, my Child, and remember what I said. You have to face your life ahead. Now go, my Child, and don’t you ever stray, Lest you waste your precious life away. Remember, I’ll be there for you, always beside you. Now go, my Child, and cross this journey through.

She is.

She’s not just that selfless mother Whose love and care exceed any other. She’s that fiery soldier of iron-will Who endures the war but stays strong still. She’s not just the lady of the house, Helping and caring to children and spouse. She’s that genius brain toiling away Behind the test tubes in the lab all day. She’s not just that teacher, gentle and wise Guiding you always with her precious advice. She’s also that student sitting apart Intently studying with soul and heart. She’s also that actress gathering fame. She’s that musician making a name. She’s that dancer, filled with utter grace. She’s that astronaut heading for space. She’s the one you see training for sport. She’s that lawyer presenting to the court. She’s that politician moving the crowd. She’s herself and she’s making us proud.

I See You

I see you, curious and afraid, I see you, helpless and weak. I hold out my hand for you You take it, humble and meek. I walk you through the jungles dense. I steer you through the waters deep. I feed you with my own two hands and Hold you in my lap to sleep. My Child! But you must learn to live And thrive, now, on your own. I can’t be there for you always Now, you must learn to walk alone. I see you now; you’re still a child. You need my hand, you’ve not grown. I see that I’m now dying of age. And soon, you’ll be all alone. Alas, my Child! If you had been A little independent of my aid, You would’ve survived when I am gone, If you’d ventured out of my shade. If you had grown into a man today, You might’ve even looked after me. You could’ve have healed your mother, but now You’re just a child, I see.

Trickle of Time

What has been, has been. What will be, will be. I can’t affect it, And it doesn’t affect me. I don’t celebrate and rejoice, Nor weep and cry For what is yet to be, And what has gone by. I am what I am, Not what I was or will be. And I am trying still To be better than me. In this ludic trickle of time, What can I certainly say? Not what was or will be But what is still, today. Neither then, nor then, Only now have I the sail. I am on the right path, but Not lost in the then gale. For what has been, has been. What will be, will be. But what is, now, must be Truly lived by me.

Manan Garg
